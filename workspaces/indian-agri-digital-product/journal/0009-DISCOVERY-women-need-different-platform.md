# DISCOVERY: Women Farmers Need a Fundamentally Different Platform, Not an Addition

## The Problem with "Women Are In Scope"

The user said "women are definitely in the scope." This is the correct intent. But adding women to a platform designed for male farmers is not inclusion — it's inadequate.

**The current approach** (adding women to a male farmer platform):
- Woman registers as "farmer"
- Receives same recommendations as male farmer
- Uses same interface (app, WhatsApp, IVR)
- Relies on same market channels

**This doesn't work** because women's farming reality is fundamentally different.

---

## How Women's Farming Reality Is Different

### Land and Tenure

| Aspect | Male Farmer | Women Farmer |
|---|---|---|
| Land ownership | In own name | In husband's/male relative's name |
| Decision-making | Sole or shared | Often secondary to male household head |
| Tenure security | Owner or formal lease | Informal, oral leases common |
| Platform access | Via land records | Excluded from land-record-based schemes |

**Implication**: Women farmers may not appear in land record databases. AgriStack integration excludes them.

### Access to Inputs

| Input | Male Access | Women Access |
|---|---|---|
| Credit | KCC in own name | Often joint, male is primary |
| Seeds/fertilizer | Can visit dealer independently | Requires male family member |
| Government schemes | Can approach office | Mobility/social barriers |
| Information | Mobile, TV, dealer | SHG meetings, peer networks |

**Implication**: Input recommendations that require going to a dealer need a female intermediary.

### Market Access

| Market Aspect | Male Access | Women Access |
|---|---|---|
| Physical market | Can visit mandi | Cannot visit distant mandi (social norms) |
| Arthiya relationship | Direct, established | Through male family member |
| Price negotiation | Can negotiate directly | May not negotiate directly |
| Transport | Can drive, arrange transport | Mobility constraints |

**Implication**: Price intelligence and market recommendations must reach women directly, not require market visits.

### Labor and Time

| Aspect | Male Perspective | Women Perspective |
|---|---|---|
| Labor accounting | Hired labor is a COST | Family labor is FREE (invisible) |
| Work burden | Agricultural work only | Agriculture + domestic work + childcare |
| Time use | Peaks in season | Flattened across year + peaks |
| Technology adoption | Labor-saving for MEN | Labor-increasing for women (milking, processing) |

**Implication**: "Labor-saving" crops may shift labor burden to women. Crop recommendations must account for who does the work.

## A Different Platform for Women Farmers

### Platform Design Changes

**1. Organizing unit: SHG, not individual**

Women farmers are reached through Self-Help Groups (SHGs), not as individuals.

- Platform organizes around SHG, not individual farmer
- SHG leader receives information, disseminates to members
- Group accountability replaces individual record-keeping
- Group credit (SHG-bank linkage) is the credit access pathway

**2. Female field staff as primary channel**

- Male field staff cannot interact with women farmers without social friction
- Female agripreneurs, SHG federations, women KVK scientists
- Platform must be accessible THROUGH female intermediaries

**3. Household-level consent model**

- Women may not have individual phone/mobile in their name
- Consent for data sharing must work when phone is shared/male-owned
- Household-level model: husband and wife both access same phone account

**4. Voice-first, audio-based**

- Women have lower literacy rates in rural areas
- Audio content (WhatsApp voice messages, IVR) is preferred
- Visual content must be minimal and simple

**5. Scheme access through cultivation rights, not land ownership**

- Women cultivate land but aren't on records
- Cultivator certificate (where available) as proof of farming
- Platform must flag women farmers as "cultivator without land title"

### Content Differences

| Content Area | Male Farmer | Women Farmer |
|---|---|---|
| Crop recommendations | Market price optimization | Household food security |
| Input recommendations | Cost minimization | Quality + accessibility |
| Market access | Best price + storage | Local processing, direct sale |
| Scheme access | Land-based schemes | Cultivator-based schemes |
| Credit | KCC, farm loans | SHG credit, dairy income |

## What This Means for the 3 Engines

### Engine 1 (Soil): Gated Differently

Women farmers are more likely to:
- Have smaller plots (0.2-0.5 ha)
- Grow vegetables and food crops (not commodity crops)
- Use organic inputs from own compost
- NOT be able to afford chemical fertilizers anyway

**Recommendation approach**: Focus on kitchen garden, nutrition garden, small livestock integration — not commercial soil optimization.

### Engine 2 (Climate): Simplified + Female-Relevant

Women farmers face:
- Different climate vulnerabilities (water collection burden increases with drought)
- Different adaptive capacity (less mobility to escape heat)
- Different crop sensitivity (vegetables more perishable)

**Recommendation approach**: Fewer, simpler advisories. Focus on water management (women's domain), food security crops, livestock integration.

### Engine 3 (Income): Household, Not Individual

Women rarely control the sale of the main crop (men do). But women DO control:
- Small livestock (poultry, goats)
- Dairy (if applicable)
- Kitchen garden surplus
- Handicrafts, wage labor

**Recommendation approach**: "Women income streams" — help women optimize what they actually control, rather than assuming they control the main crop income.

---

## The Honest Assessment

**Building a women farmer platform is a different product, not an addition to the male farmer platform.**

If women are truly in scope, the platform needs:
1. Separate user journey (SHG-based, not individual)
2. Female field staff network
3. Different content and recommendations
4. Different scheme pathways (cultivator rights)
5. Different income optimization (what women control)

**This requires partnerships with SHG organizations, women FPOs, and women's federations** — not just feature additions.
