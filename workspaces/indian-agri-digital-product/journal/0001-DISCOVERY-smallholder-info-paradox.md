# DISCOVERY: The Information Paradox is a Red Herring

## Finding

The research revealed a counterintuitive finding that challenges the core premise of most "digital agriculture" interventions: **Information is not the binding constraint on farmer welfare.**

### The Evidence
- 40+ government portals exist (soil health cards, PM-KISAN, e-NAM, PMFBY)
- Soil Health Cards issued to 230M farmers — but only 15-18% usage
- PMFBY enrollment collapsed from 30M to 13.5M despite government push
- Only 12% of farmers receive weather advisories — not because they don't exist, but because delivery fails

### Why This Matters
Most digital agriculture products assume: "If we give farmers better information, they'll make better decisions and improve their welfare."

**This assumption is wrong for most farmers.** The binding constraints are:
- **Credit interlocking**: 45% of agricultural debt from informal sources at 24-100% interest rates. Moneylender dictates crop choices.
- **MSP infrastructure**: 85-90% of procurement for rice/wheat. No procurement pathway for millets.
- **Storage deficit**: 50% cold storage deficit. Farmers must sell immediately.
- **Land lease restrictions**: Tenants can't access schemes or credit.

### Implication for Product
The product must NOT be framed as "information platform." Instead:
- Information can only create value for the 15-20% of farmers who have relative freedom from these constraints
- For the rest, the product must bundle information with structural interventions (storage access, credit alternatives, FPO linkages)
- Or target the institutional buyers who CAN act on information (banks, processors)
