# DISCOVERY: The Buyer-User Mismatch is a Structural, Not Tactical, Problem

## Finding

The value audit identified that "the buyer is not the user" — farmers can't pay, institutions pay but have misaligned incentives. This isn't a product design problem that can be designed around. It's a **structural feature of Indian agricultural markets.**

### Why This Matters for Product Design

Most startup thinking: "Find the pain point, build the solution, charge for it."

In Indian agriculture:
- The pain is real (farmer poverty, low income, volatility)
- But the person who feels the pain (farmer) can't pay
- And the person who CAN pay (institution) doesn't feel the pain directly

**The institution's pain is:**
- Bank: NPA on agricultural loans
- Input company: Inability to reach smallholders profitably
- Processor: Can't guarantee quality/traceability
- Government: Scheme exclusion errors, political optics

**These pains are real but INDIRECTLY related to farmer welfare.** A bank wants to reduce NPA, not maximize farmer income.

### The B2B2F Workaround

B2B2F works IF:
1. Institution pays for farmer access/engagement (not farmer welfare improvement)
2. Farmer welfare is an OUTPUT of the institution getting what they want
3. The metrics are aligned enough that both parties benefit

**The risk:** Institution optimizes for their metric (NPA reduction, sales volume) at the expense of farmer welfare. The platform becomes a tool for extracting value from farmers, not improving it.

### Mitigation Design Principle

The platform must have **farmer welfare guardrails** — even if institution pays, certain outcomes are unacceptable:
- No data sharing that leads to predatory lending
- No scheme enrollment that harms farmer's eligibility for other schemes
- No price manipulation that harms farmers
