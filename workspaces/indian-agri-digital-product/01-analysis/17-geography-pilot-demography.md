## Executive Summary

**Recommendation:** Launch Phase 1 pilots in **Maharashtra (Nashik + Ahmednagar districts)** and **Karnataka (Dharwad + Belgaum districts)** in parallel. These two states combine the highest concentration of commercially-oriented smallholders with actionable Income Engine relevance, existing institutional infrastructure (FPOs, KVKs, RRBs), adequate mobile penetration for audio/WhatsApp delivery, and climate exposure that creates Phase 3 readiness. Rajasthan (Bikaner + Jodhpur) is the secondary option for a rainfed/foodgrains variant.

**Complexity: Moderate** — choices are constrained by the CPE gate architecture and the B2B2F revenue model. Key risk: pilot success depends on institutional partnerships (FPOs, KVKs, RRBs) that require 3-6 months to formalize before any farmer-facing deployment.

## 1. Geography Recommendation

These states are worth revisiting in Phase 4, not Phase 1.

### 1.3 Primary Recommendation: Maharashtra

**Why Maharashtra:**
- **Income Engine relevance is highest:** Onion prices exhibit the most extreme volatility documented in Indian agriculture (farmgate 3-5 rupees/kg during export ban crashes vs. 60-80 rupees/kg retail). Grape farmers face similar dynamics with export market dependency. The Income Engine's selling decision module has the clearest use case here.
- **FPO ecosystem is operational:** Maharashtra has the highest number of active FPOs in the country (~800+ registered FPOs, of which ~200-300 are operational with active member engagement). FPOs provide the B2B2F institutional anchor needed for Phase 1 revenue model validation.
- **Climate risk exposure:** Marathwada region (Ahmednagar,奥斯) has documented recurring drought cycles. Nashik faces occasional cyclone exposure from Bay of Bengal. This creates Phase 3 Climate Engine relevance without needing to establish a greenfield.
- **Digital infrastructure:** 4G coverage in target districts is reliable; WhatsApp usage is high among farmers who have smartphones; Kisan Call Centre in Marathi is functional.
- **State government posture:** Maharashtra's agricultural digital programs (Mahadwara) are operational, though the relationship with a private platform does not require state partnership — the FPO/KVK channel is sufficient.

**Districts recommended:** Nashik and Ahmednagar in parallel.

| District | Primary Crops | Why This District | Key Partners |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nashik** | Grapes (table + wine), onion, wheat | Highest commercial crop diversity; grape export market creates real price-timing decisions; onion price volatility is the Income Engine's best use case; strong FPO presence (Mahagrapes) | Mahagrapes FPO, Nashik District KVK, HDFC Bank rural branch |
| **Ahmednagar** | Onion, soybean, wheat, milk (dairy) | Drought-prone; strongest case for Income Engine to reduce distress sales; high tenant share (Gate 2 relevance); dairy adds cash flow stability dimension | Sheti Mazo Kisan FPO, Ahmednagar KVK, Maharashtra RRB |

**CPE gate filtering implication:** Ahmednagar's higher tenant share means Gate 2 (Tenure) will suppress Soil multi-year recommendations for a larger share of enrolled farmers in that district. This is a feature — it validates the CPE's gate filtering at scale.

### 1.4 Secondary Recommendation: Karnataka

**Why Karnataka:**
- **Stronger digital infrastructure baseline:** Karnataka's rural smartphone penetration and internet reliability exceed Maharashtra's average. The Karnataka Digital Economy Mission creates a more favorable institutional environment.
- **Agricultural university network:** University of Agricultural Sciences, Dharwad (UAS-D) and its Krishi Vigyan Kendra network provides extension services infrastructure that Maharashtra lacks in comparable density.
- **Crop profile:** Onion (Dharwad/Belgaum are major onion belts), soybean (Karnataka is India's second-largest producer), maize, and sugarcane — all commercially oriented with mandi price exposure.
- **FPO maturity:** Karnataka FPOs, while fewer in number than Maharashtra, tend toward higher organizational maturity and financial sustainability.
- **Phase 3 readiness:** Karnataka's drought-prone districts (Dharwad, Bagalkote, Koppal) have documented groundwater depletion and rainfall variability, creating the Climate Engine use case.

**Districts recommended:** Dharwad and Belgaum in parallel.

| District | Primary Crops | Why This District | Key Partners |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dharwad** | Onion, soybean, maize, groundnut | Strong smallholder concentration; documented price volatility in onion; UAS-D KVK provides extension backbone; RRB (Karnataka Gramin Bank) presence | Dharwad KVK, University of Agricultural Sciences Dharwad, Syngenta-backed FPO initiative |
| **Belgaum** | Onion, soybean, sugarcane, cotton | Largest district by population in Karnataka; high smallholder density; Belgaum District has mixed cropping systems relevant to Income Engine across crops | Belgaum KVK, Maharashtra-Karnataka border FPO network |

### 1.5 Tertiary Option: Rajasthan (Bikaner + Jodhpur)

If the primary geographies fail to yield institutional partnership commitments within 6 months, Rajasthan's Bikaner and Jodhpur districts offer a foodgrains/mustard variant:

- Strong RRB (Barmer RRB, Jodhpur RRB) penetration — banking channel as B2B2F anchor
- Mustard and wheat have meaningful MSP price exposure
- Climate risk (drought, heat) is extreme — validates Phase 3 climate engine
- Lower mobile penetration requires IVR-primary delivery (not WhatsApp)

### 1.6 Why Not Bihar, UP, Jharkhand

The value audit (document 06) explicitly recommends excluding states with high tenant populations and weak mandi infrastructure. Bihar and Jharkhand fail both tests:

- **Bihar:** APMC Act repealed (2006); farmers sell to informal traders at farmgate, not mandis. No price discovery mechanism exists. The Income Engine's mandi price module has nothing to optimize against.
- **UP (Eastern):** Weak mandi infrastructure outside Western UP; very high tenant share; low mobile penetration.
- **Jharkhand:** Tribal-dominated, rainfed, very low commercial orientation; digital infrastructure insufficient.

These states are Phase 4 or later candidates when the product has institutional partnerships that can operate independently of mandi infrastructure.

## 2. Pilot Strategy

### 2.1 Village Selection Criteria

Villages must satisfy ALL of the following for Phase 1 inclusion:

| Criterion | Threshold | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **4G mobile coverage** | Reliable signal (no dead zones) in village center | Required for IVR/WhatsApp delivery; non-negotiable |
| **At least one active FPO or SHG group** | With 50+ member farmers | B2B2F institutional anchor; FPO staff as channel intermediaries |
| **At least one KVK or agribusiness touchpoint** | Within 30km or accessible via regular visits | Extension services backbone; trust intermediary |
| **Crop commerciality** | >50% of cultivated area in crops with mandi price exposure (onion, soybean, wheat, grapes, cotton, maize) | Income Engine relevance |
| **No more than 80% owner-cultivators** | Tenant share >20% preferred | Tests Gate 2 (Tenure) filtering at operational scale |
| **Population 500-2,000 households** | Village is small enough to enumerate; large enough for statistical validity | Operational feasibility for field agents |

**Village exclusion criteria (hard stops):**
- No mobile coverage (cannot be remediated within pilot timeline)
- Village has active predatory arthiya relationships covering >80% of farmers (credit interlocking is too complete for the Income Engine to provide incremental value within Phase 1)
- Landholding is >60% medium/large (>4ha) — not the target demographic

**Selection process:** Overlay village-level data from Digital Infrastructure analysis (document 03) with district agricultural officer records, KVK outreach lists, and FPO membership rolls. Cross-reference with SHC distribution data to estimate landholding structure. Verify mobile coverage via field agent reconnaissance or TRAI's village connectivity maps.

### 2.2 Sample Size Rationale

**Statistical validity floor:** To detect a 15% improvement in selling price realization (the primary Income Engine outcome metric) with 80% power at 95% confidence, the minimum sample is approximately **400 farmers per district** (assuming 30% coefficient of variation in selling prices, the typical range in onion markets).

**Operational feasibility ceiling:** One field agent can meaningfully manage 150-200 active farmers during the first 6 months (high-touch onboarding, trust building, constraint state data collection). With 4 field agents per district, the operational ceiling is **600-800 farmers per district**.

**Recommended Phase 1 sample:** 500 farmers per district across 4 districts = **2,000 farmers total**, with 3-5 villages per district. This satisfies both the statistical floor and the operational ceiling.

| Layer | Specification |
|---|---|
| Farmers per village | 100-170 (4-5 villages per district) |
| Villages per district | 3-5 |
| Districts (parallel) | 2 (Nashik + Ahmednagar OR Dharwad + Belgaum) |
| Total farmers enrolled | 2,000 (1,000 per state) |
| Active farmers (Monthly Active) target at month 6 | 60% = 1,200 |

**Enrollment buffer:** Enroll 2,500 farmers to account for 20% attrition (migration, disinterest, constraint state suppression). Analysis is conducted on the 2,000 active cohort.

### 2.3 Pilot Duration

**Minimum:** 12 months — one complete kharif + rabi crop cycle. The Income Engine's selling recommendation module is only testable during an actual harvest and selling window. A pilot that starts in April misses the rabi wheat harvest; one starting in October misses kharif. Timing must align with a sowing season start.

**Recommended:** 18 months — covers two full crop cycles and validates that the CPE's constraint state updates work across seasons (cash flow status transitions, tenure type changes, health status reporting).

**Recommended start window:** June (kharif sowing season). This aligns with:
- Kharif crop planting decisions (Income Engine Module 1: input cost optimization)
- Full kharif season coverage including harvest (Income Engine Module 4: selling decision)
- Rabi planning in October-November (second cycle validation)
- End of pilot: December Month 18 (rabi harvest 2)

### 2.4 Success Metrics

Success must be measured at three levels: **farmer outcomes**, **platform performance**, and **institutional partnership viability**.

#### Farmer Outcome Metrics (PRIMARY)

| Metric | Measurement Method | Target | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Selling price improvement** | Compare farmer-reported selling price vs. district average mandi price (agmarknet) for same crop, same week | +10-15% improvement | Month 12 |
| **Distress sale reduction** | Share of farmers selling within 7 days of harvest (distress window) vs. pilot baseline | 20% reduction in distress sales | Month 12 |
| **Cash flow stability** | Income Engine Module 2 weekly cash flow status; share of farmers in "surplus" or "balanced" vs. "deficit" or "emergency" | 15% improvement in cash flow status | Month 18 |
| **Recommendation action rate** | Share of farmers who act on a CPE-passed recommendation within the recommended window | >60% action rate | Month 6 |
| **Suppression notification comprehension** | Share of farmers who, upon receiving a suppression notification, correctly explain why the recommendation was suppressed | >50% comprehension | Month 6 |

#### Platform Performance Metrics

| Metric | Measurement Method | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **Monthly Active Farmers (MAF)** | Platform analytics: farmers who received or interacted with at least one recommendation in 30-day window | >60% of enrolled at Month 6; >75% at Month 12 |
| **Recommendation coverage** | Share of enrolled farmers who received at least one CPE-passed recommendation (vs. suppression) in the period | >70% at Month 12 |
| **Gate 2 (Tenure) accuracy** | Cross-validate self-reported tenure_type against field agent observations; error rate | <15% |
| **Gate 3 (Cash Flow) accuracy** | Cross-validate Income Engine Module 2 cash flow status against farmer self-reported expenditure in 90-day recall survey; error rate | <20% |
| **False suppression rate** | Share of suppression notifications where the farmer self-reports they could have acted (against field agent validation) | <10% |

#### Institutional Partnership Metrics

| Metric | Measurement Method | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **FPO engagement depth** | Share of FPOs that actively use the field agent dashboard and provide farmer feedback loop | >60% of enrolled FPOs |
| **KVK co-delivery** | Number of KVK-organized farmer meetings where the Income Engine recommendation framework was presented | >1 per district per quarter |
| **B2B2F contract intent** | At least one FPO or RRB signs a letter of intent or pilot agreement for Phase 2 cost-sharing | 1 signed LOI per state by Month 12 |
| **Field agent productivity** | Cost per active farmer month (field agent salary + travel + device costs / active farmers served) | <Rs 150/farmer/month at Month 12 |

### 2.5 Phase 1 Pilot to Phase 2 Scale Playbook

**Exit criteria from Phase 1 pilot to Phase 2 scale:**

1. Selling price improvement of at least +10% demonstrated in at least one district
2. Monthly Active Farmer rate >55% sustained for 3 consecutive months
3. Recommendation action rate >55%
4. At least one institutional partner (FPO or RRB) signed LOI for Phase 2
5. No Gate 2/Gate 3 accuracy errors above 25% (validation that constraint state data quality is sufficient for Phase 2 Soil Engine integration)

**Phase 2 expansion (Months 12-24):**

- **Scale within Maharashtra:** Add 3 districts (Pune, Solapur, Jalgaon) — same FPO/KVK institutional model; onion + grape cropping system; existing relationships reduce onboarding cost
- **Scale within Karnataka:** Add 3 districts (Bagalkote, Koppal, Gadag) — drought-prone, strong FPO presence; soybean + onion
- **New state entry:** Rajasthan (Bikaner + Jodhpur) if Phase 1 demonstrated B2B2F model works and institutional partnership is formalized
- **Target Phase 2 farmer base:** 8,000-12,000 farmers across 10-12 districts

**Phase 3 expansion (Months 24-36):**

- Add Climate Engine (district-level advisories) to existing geographies
- Enter Madhya Pradesh (Indore + Ujjain) with soybean + wheat focus
- Target: 25,000-40,000 farmers

**Phase 4 (Month 36+):**
- Full integration (Soil micro-zoning + Climate at district level + Income Engine)
- Enter Uttar Pradesh (Western UP: Meerut + Muzaffarnagar) with sugarcane + wheat

## 3. Target Demographics at Start

### 3.1 Farm Size Segmentation

| Category | Size | Share of All Holdings | Phase 1 Relevance | CPE Gate Implications |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Marginal** | <1 ha | ~70% | Primary target for Income Engine | Gate 3 (Cash Flow) fires frequently — emergency/deficit cash flow suppresses capital-intensive recommendations |
| **Small** | 1-2 ha | ~16% | Core target for Income Engine | Gate 3 fires less frequently; more likely to have marketable surplus |
| **Semi-medium** | 2-4 ha | ~9% | Secondary target | Less price volatile; fewer distress sales; lower Income Engine relevance per farmer |
| **Medium/Large** | >4 ha | ~5% | Excluded from primary enrollment | Not the addressable segment; income volatility is lower |

**Phase 1 targeting:** Enroll farmers with 0.5-2.5 ha operational holding. This captures the "most addressable" segment — enough land to generate marketable surplus for mandi selling, enough smallholder economics to face meaningful price volatility.

**Why not focus on the bottom 70% (marginal <1ha) exclusively:** The value audit correctly identifies that marginal farmers often face such severe credit interlocking and immediate cash needs that the Income Engine's selling recommendation cannot overcome the structural constraint. The 0.5-2.5 ha band includes farmers who have some agency in selling decisions while still representing the smallholder majority.

### 3.2 Tenure Segmentation

| Tenure Type | Share of Cultivating Households | CPE Gate 2 Implication | Phase 1 Enrollment Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Owner-cultivator** | ~55-60% | No suppression for any Soil recommendation | Eligible; target ~60% of enrollment |
| **Tenant farmer (leased-in land)** | ~20-25% | Multi-year soil investments suppressed; season-level interventions pass | Eligible; target ~35% of enrollment; explicitly test Gate 2 filtering |
| **Sharecropper** | ~10-15% | Same as tenant; often informal lease | Eligible if operating >0.5ha; target ~5% |
| **Landless laborer** | ~25-30% | Out of scope — no land to make cropping decisions | Excluded |

**Gate 2 (Tenure) importance for pilot design:** The pilot must explicitly include tenant farmers at the 30-35% level to validate that Gate 2 filtering works correctly. If the pilot enrolls only owner-cultivators, Phase 2 Soil Engine integration will be untested for the tenant use case. Ahmednagar district's documented higher tenant share makes it the better district for Gate 2 validation.

**Tenant detection protocol:** Self-report at onboarding (land_tenure field in farmer profile). Flag as "unverified." Accept self-report for Phase 1; formalize against land records (where digitized) in Phase 2. Where land records are absent (Bihar, Jharkhand, parts of UP), tenant status is accepted on the basis of self-report plus field agent attestation.

### 3.3 Gender Strategy

**The brief explicitly includes women farmers as in-scope.** The digital infrastructure analysis (document 03) documents that female farmers have only 25-30% smartphone penetration and 10-15% smartphone operational capability. A "download the app" strategy excludes most women farmers by design.

**Women farmer engagement strategy for Phase 1:**

| Channel | Mechanism | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **SHG (Self-Help Group) linkage** | Partner with Mahila SHG federations (in Maharashtra) and Stree Shakti SHGs (in Karnataka) | SHGs provide a trusted all-women institution; group dynamics overcome individual digital literacy gaps |
| **IVR (interactive voice response)** | Hosted telephone hotline in Marathi/Kannada; women call in to receive recommendation playback and report harvest status | No smartphone required; voice in local language; group listening sessions common |
| **Field agent (female)** | Deploy at least one female field agent per district; target women farmers through SHG meetings and home visits | Trust intermediary; women farmers are more likely to report health status (Gate 1) to a female agent |
| **WhatsApp (group)** | Create women-farmer WhatsApp groups through SHG leaders; recommendations forwarded by SHG sanchit (coordinator) | Only viable for the ~25-30% of women with smartphones; SHG group model extends reach |
| **Husbands as delivery channel** | Where women do not have direct phone access, deliver recommendations via the household's male phone holder with explicit permission | Must be explicitly opted into by women farmers; not assumed |

**Enrollment target:** Women farmers should represent at least **30% of pilot enrollment** (1,500 of 5,000 target across 18 months). Maharashtra's SHG ecosystem (supported by DAY-NRLM/Mahila Arthik Evam Samajik Sanvardhan Mandal — MAHIM) provides the institutional infrastructure to achieve this.

**Specific women farmer metrics:**

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Share of women farmers in total enrollment | >30% |
| Women farmer MAU (monthly active) rate | >50% (lower than male due to channel constraints) |
| Women farmer recommendation action rate | >50% |
| Share of women farmers receiving suppression notification | Proportional to tenant/cash flow share |

### 3.4 Language Strategy

**Phase 1 languages:** Marathi (Maharashtra) and Kannada (Kannada) as primary languages. Hindi as secondary for migrant farmer populations.

**Why Marathi and Kannada:**

- Both states have strong linguistic identity; farmers strongly prefer mother-tongue delivery
- Kisan Call Centre operates in both languages (proves technical feasibility)
- IVR content production in both languages is achievable within pilot budget (6-8 weeks of content development)
- Field agents must be native speakers or demonstrate fluency at B2+ level (CEFR)

**Language deployment priority:**

| Priority | Language | State | Channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | Marathi | Maharashtra | IVR, WhatsApp, field agent |
| 1st | Kannada | Karnataka | IVR, WhatsApp, field agent |
| 2nd | Hindi | Both states | WhatsApp (text), field agent |
| 3rd | English | Both states | App (only, for educated progressive farmers) — optional |

**Tribal language contingency:** In districts with >15% tribal population (e.g., Nashik's tribal talukas: Kalwan, Surgana; Dharwad's border areas), provide field agent support in Marathi/Kannada as the bridging language, with tribal language interpreters recruited from KVK tribal outreach programs. Do not invest in full IVR content in tribal languages during Phase 1.

## 4. Operational Considerations

### 4.1 Field Agent Model

**Phase 1 field model:** High-touch human intermediation. One field agent per 150-200 enrolled farmers during the first 6 months, scaling to 1 per 250-300 by Month 12 as digital channels mature.

**Field agent responsibilities:**
1. Farmer enrollment and constraint state data collection (land tenure, health status, crop cycle dates, selling window reporting)
2. CPE recommendation delivery and explanation (in person or via phone for non-smartphone farmers)
3. Ground truth data collection (actual selling prices, selling dates, mandi visited, intermediate prices received)
4. Feedback loop back to platform (farmer pushback on recommendations, suppression notification comprehension)
5. Institutional relationship management (FPO, KVK, RRB point of contact)

**Field agent profile:**
- Agricultural university graduate (BSC Agriculture minimum) or diploma holder from agricultural polytechnic
- Native language speaker for the district
- Owns a smartphone with 4G data plan (reimbursed as part of compensation)
- Prior experience in extension services (KVK, input company field force, FPO staff) preferred
- Female candidates strongly preferred for women farmer engagement

**Field agent density:**

| Phase | Months | Farmers per Field Agent | Field Agents per District |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 pilot launch | Months 1-6 | 150 | 6-7 |
| Pilot consolidation | Months 7-12 | 200 | 5 |
| Phase 2 scale | Months 13-18 | 250 | 8-10 |
| Phase 2 scale | Months 19-24 | 300 | 6-7 |

**Field agent compensation structure:** Monthly retainer (Rs 15,000-18,000) + performance bonus tied to MAU rate and recommendation action rate. Not tied to product sales or institutional partnership outcomes (avoids conflicts of interest).

### 4.2 Channel Priorities

**Channel hierarchy for Phase 1:**

| Priority | Channel | Target Segment | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1st** | IVR (voice hotline) | All farmers (primary) | No smartphone required; voice in local language; asynchronous; works with shared phones |
| **2nd** | WhatsApp (text + audio) | Women farmers via SHG; smartphone owners | Group delivery extends reach; audio messages overcome low literacy |
| **3rd** | Field agent visit/call | New enrollments; suppression notifications; complex recommendations | High-trust; enables constraint state data collection |
| **4th** | USSD (optional) | Farmers with basic phone, no smartphone | Fallback; low functionality; keep minimal investment |
| **5th** | Mobile app | Progressive/commercial farmers with smartphones (>20% of enrolled) | Future growth channel; Phase 1 app is for field agent use primarily |

**Channel investment ratio (Phase 1 budget allocation):**

| Channel | % of Operations Budget |
|---|---|
| IVR platform (hosted, per-minute cost) | 25% |
| WhatsApp Business API + content | 15% |
| Field agent salaries + travel | 45% |
| App development/maintenance | 10% |
| USSD (minimal) | 5% |

**IVR protocol design:** The IVR system is not a broadcast channel. It is a pull system: farmers call in (toll-free number) to receive their personalized recommendation playback, report harvest status, or query selling window dates. Outbound calls (recommendation notifications) are made by field agents via their mobile phones, not automated IVR broadcasts. This keeps the system simple and avoids TRAI spam-call compliance complications.

### 4.3 Data Collection During Pilot

The pilot is not just a product validation — it is a ground truth data collection exercise that enables Phase 2 (Soil micro-zoning) and Phase 3 (Climate Engine district-level advisories).

**Data collection priorities:**

| Data Layer | Collection Method | Phase 1 Deliverable | Phase 2/3 Dependence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Farmer constraint state** | Onboarding survey by field agent | Validated CPE gate accuracy rates | Soil Engine integration |
| **Crop cycle dates** | Farmer self-report + field agent confirmation | Selling window timing database | Climate Engine timing |
| **Actual selling prices** | Field agent recall survey at 7, 30, 60 days post-harvest | Selling price benchmark dataset | Income Engine Module 4 calibration |
| **Mandi price vs. farmgate price spread** | Agmarknet data cross-referenced with farmer reports | Price realization gap analysis | Income Engine Module 3 calibration |
| **Cash flow patterns** | Farmer expenditure recall survey (90-day); field agent observation | Cash flow status distribution | Gate 3 (Cash Flow) calibration |
| **Soil self-assessment** | Farmer填报 (paper form, digitized by field agent) | Plot-level soil type + irrigation source | Phase 2 Soil micro-zoning |
| **Weather outcomes** | Farmer-reported extreme weather events (flood, drought, unseasonal rain) | District-level climate event log | Phase 3 Climate Engine validation |
| **Crop yield** | Field agent crop cut estimate + farmer self-report | Yield per hectare by plot | Soil Engine + Climate Engine correlation |

**Ground truth data governance:** All farmer data collected is anonymized before analysis. Individual farm-level data is not shared with institutional partners (FPOs, KVKs, RRBs) without explicit farmer consent. Aggregate district-level data may be shared for Phase 2 partnership discussions.

## 5. CPE Gate Implications for Pilot Design

### 5.1 Gate 1 (Health) — Impact on Pilot

Health status = crisis suppresses all recommendations. The pilot must establish a health status reporting mechanism through field agents (not rely on farmer self-report via IVR, which is unreliable for sensitive health information).

**Pilot design implication:** Field agents are the Gate 1 data collection mechanism. A farmer reporting a household medical emergency to a field agent triggers the health_gate update. This requires field agent training on health status criteria and a reporting protocol.

### 5.2 Gate 2 (Tenure) — Impact on Pilot

Tenant farmers are explicitly included in enrollment. Gate 2 must correctly suppress multi-year Soil recommendations while passing season-level interventions. The pilot must validate that the tenure_type field accurately reflects farmer circumstances — informal lease agreements are common and self-reported tenure may not match ground truth.

**Pilot design implication:** The pilot should measure false-negative rate for Gate 2 (tenant farmers marked as owners, and vice versa). An error rate >20% on tenure classification would require a Gate 2 accuracy improvement initiative before Phase 2 Soil Engine integration.

### 5.3 Gate 3 (Cash Flow) — Impact on Pilot

Cash flow status = emergency/deficit suppresses capital-intensive recommendations. The Income Engine Module 2 cash flow assessment must be calibrated against actual farmer expenditure patterns during the pilot. The pilot should include a 90-day recall survey at enrollment and at 6 months to cross-validate cash flow status accuracy.

**Pilot design implication:** The pilot should establish the share of enrolled farmers in each cash flow status category. If >50% of farmers are in "deficit" or "emergency" status, the pilot must adjust its success metrics — the Income Engine's impact on selling decisions may be constrained by Gate 3 for a majority of the cohort.

### 5.4 Gate 4 (Selling Window) — Impact on Pilot

This gate is the most directly testable during the pilot. The Income Engine's selling decision module must issue recommendations that are suppressed correctly during the harvest window (selling recommendations pass; soil recommendations suppressed). The pilot should track the ratio of passed vs. suppressed recommendations by gate during the harvest season.

**Pilot design implication:** The pilot harvest season data (months 4-6 and months 16-18) is the primary validation event for Gate 4. Field agents must report actual harvest dates to validate selling_window = open timing.

### 5.5 Gate 5 (Time Criticality) — Impact on Pilot

Time criticality (sowing_window, growing_window, harvest_window) requires accurate crop cycle date data. The pilot's data collection on sowing dates and expected harvest dates feeds Gate 5. This gate is only fully testable across two crop cycles.

## 6. Risk Register

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Institutional partnerships (FPO, KVK) do not materialize | Medium | Pilot cannot reach farmer enrollment targets | Begin partnership discussions 6 months before pilot launch; identify 2-3 FPO candidates per district |
| Gate 2 (Tenure) self-report accuracy is too low | Medium | Soil Engine Phase 2 integration is compromised | Deploy field agent ground-truthing for a 10% random sample; set accuracy threshold at 80% |
| Women farmer enrollment below 30% target | High | Brief requirement unmet | SHG partnership must be secured before pilot launch; female field agents are non-negotiable |
| Gate 3 (Cash Flow) fires for >60% of farmers | Medium | Most farmers receive only selling recommendations; platform appears feature-poor | Reframe success metrics to focus on selling price improvement rather than recommendation diversity |

| Field agent attrition >30% in first 6 months | Medium | Operational cost per farmer spikes | Competitive compensation; performance bonus structure; career path visibility |
| e-NAM price data unavailable for Karnataka pilot districts | Low-Medium | Income Engine Module 3 (Mandi Bargaining Visibility) has no data source | Negotiate agmarknet API access with DAC&FW before pilot; have manual price reporting fallback |
